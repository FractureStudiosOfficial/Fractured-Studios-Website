DEVLOG_001: The Iron Frontier!

[DEVLOG] [IRON_FRONTIER] [SYSTEM] [INIT]

Hello! My name is Liam, the founder and creator of Fractured Studios. Here is a bit about me. Currently I am a studying Astrophysicist, with plans of becoming an Astrobiologist! Now thats a bit of a strange combination, but there is some explanation here. I have for the longest time LOVED space, and video games, and story telling in general. My first story was going to be a trilogy of movies, which is a long story that I will likely tell in the future. Anyway, the main reason you are likely here. The Iron Frontier, I began building the idea and story for this game around june of 2025, so its been a full year now. Development has been initially slow, and that is due in large part to limited hardware. I also want to address the website. While I like the visual style, the person I commissioned it from "vibe" coded it. I would like to get another person to make it who is an actual programmer.

The idea of my first game being something rather simple that I could make, whilst demonstrating all of my skills as a 3d modeler, writer, and game dev was the main spark for making this game. Although, as I continued to develop this game and its concepts, it changed quite a bit. Slowly but surely I regained a lot of my love for space, and space travel in general. I began to get more into astronomy, and upcoming space missions, which led me to find the Artemis missions. The prospect that humanity was returning to the moon was exilerating, and I just kept learning more and more. I researched the space race, the soviets acheivements, the US moon landings, the shuttle missions, and now the Starship missions and so on.

This eventually led up to the Artemis II mission which took place in April of this year. Since then I have been GLUED to all space related news, and have been expanding the story of The Iron Frontier and the science behind it. The Iron Frontier is quickly becoming a favorite of my story creations and will likely have more to come after the initial game release. As of right now the game is still in its infacy, and has a ways to go. Realistically if I can get a better SSD for my new pc, I could see a timeline of the first demo being released MAYBE in January of 2027. With family responsibilties I have had little time to spend on the game, but as college begins I will gain more time for both my studies, friends, and The Iron Frontier.

The Current public story is readable on The Iron Frontire page through the games tab, but I will layout some of the footnotes here. Going all the way back to the Bolshevik revolution, history played differently. Stalin was killed and Bukkharin took power. In continuing Lenin's new economic deal the new country began to expand exponentially, and come the 1950's the space race started to heat up. The Soviets put Sputnik into orbit in 1956 and kicked everything into gear. Korelev began to design the N1, as a multipurpose rocket to take humans to the Moon, and then to Mars and Venus. In the US, Vaun Braun chose to build the Nova rocket, as apposed to the Saturn V. The Nova's design allowed for exponential redesigns to increase size and lifting power, a combination of what the Sea Dragon would have been.

As both superpowers focused their efforts more towards space, the window of what to do with it narrowed. The Soviets intended to put people on the Moon to both learn about it and defend the motherland from possible nuclear strikes, but as they got closer they understood it takes quite some time to get from the moon to earth. Instead the vision was shifted to exploration, and harvesting. Early Fusion research suggested the use of Helium-3 to create sustainable reactions, and as the soviets managed to perform a smash and grab of the moon, they learned it may hold more access to this rare isotope. With this knowledge they raced to show the world that not only were they superior in technology, but power as well, for no bomb could triumph what free infinate energy could create.

With better living situations, Sergei Korelev pushed the Soviets rocketry to incredible heights, with amazing advancements in just a few years. With the Americans also pushing deeper into the space race, both sides knew the importance of space. As the years ticked by, the space race employed nearly 2 million people across both superpowers, all leading up to one fateful day in 1965. So, on this day, July 18th of 1965, the first human steps foot on the moon. A soviet woman is the first to touch down on the Lunar surface, beating the americans in the first part of the Space Race. For all of Humanity, the largest collective project has began. Expanding Humans to every corner of our solar syste.

Now I will end here as I do not wish to spoil everything! I am working hard to get the demo out so everyone can experience this world. I am loving making it, and cannot wait to share it with more people. There is so much to come from this story, but all good things take time. I will see you all very soon, but for now I must get ready for college!

DEVLOG_002: WORLD_BUILDING_PROGRESS

[DEVLOG] [IRON_FRONTIER] [LORE] [WORLD]

Focus has shifted toward defining the environmental identity of our Venusian setting. Venus in our alternative 1980s history is not just a dead rock; it's a graveyard of abandoned Soviet and American research outposts. We've been designing "Outpost Kedr," the first major hub the players will explore. The architecture features heavy concrete brutalism merged with pressurized dome structures.

To convey the absolute isolation and crushing pressure of the atmosphere, we are employing a restrictive audio-visual palette. Heavy wind howling through structural fractures, the persistent hum of the life support radiator, and the clicking of Geiger counters will form the ambient soundtrack. Visually, the dense sulfuric acid fog limits visibility to mere meters, forcing players to navigate using active sonar map sweeps on their cockpit radar screens.

Lore-building is also progressing nicely. We've written over 40 logs, diary entries, and technical manuals that players can retrieve from terminal databases scattered throughout the ruins. These documents paint a picture of a desperate colonizing effort that was abruptly ordered to evacuate. The mystery of why they were left behind is the central thread players will unravel.

DEVLOG_003: COMBAT_SYSTEM_ALPHA

[DEVLOG] [IRON_FRONTIER] [COMBAT] [ALPHA] [UPDATE]

We have successfully completed our first alpha playtest of the hazard defense system (colloquially referred to as "combat"). In The Iron Frontier, combat is not about run-and-gun firefights; it is a slow, tense struggle of resource management and system routing. Players don't carry rifles — they control the lander's external automated defense turrets and internal hatch sealing mechanisms via a command-line interface.

During playtests, we observed an incredible level of tension when players had to manually input terminal commands to redirect power from life support to the secondary kinetic defense grid while hearing the metallic thuds of anomalous Venusian fauna scrambling across the hull. The typing delay adds a layer of panic that fits the CRT aesthetic perfectly.

Feedback from our three external testers has been invaluable. While they loved the tense atmosphere, we need to improve the visual feedback when systems are damaged. We are currently implementing screen-glitch effects and alarm sirens that trigger when structural integrity drops below 30%. The next phase is to refine the turret tracking algorithms and start working on the UI layout for the power grid control panel.
