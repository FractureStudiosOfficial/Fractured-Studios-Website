01

UNIVERSAL INTERSTELLAR COMPUTERS

FR-77 · Terminal Node Standard Issue · 1989
DOC.01

U.I.C. — Universal Interstellar Computers — is the standard mainframe network deployed across solar-system infrastructure. Every station, every rail, every hab-module runs on a U.I.C. node like the one you are reading this manual on. If it has a screen and it hums, it is almost certainly U.I.C.

This unit is a Model FR-77. It runs UIC OS v1.0.4 with 64K core memory, RS-232 uplink, and a cipher engine for secure link. When it boots it will run a power-on self-test and then hand you a prompt. Everything after that prompt is yours to command.

§ 01.1 — History in Brief

Humanity landed on the Moon in 1965. The first woman on the Moon planted a flag; the Soviets beat the United States there and the race never actually ended. Instead of packing up, both blocs raced to build lunar bases, orbital stations, and outposts on Venus and Mars. This is the timeline you are living in.

§ 01.2 — What We're Building

The current flagship project is the Venetian Mass Driver Rail (V.M.D.R.) — a rail system being fabricated at the Venus Surface Habitation Modules. Sister infrastructure at the Europa Mass Driver Fabrication Station supports the Europa-to-Venus water pipeline that feeds the Venetian terra-forming project. The long-range plan: three habitable planets. Earth. Mars. Venus.

This manual covers public-tier terminal operation only. Restricted-access sequences exist but are not documented here and are not available to unauthenticated users.
02

HOW TO USE THE TERMINAL

Prompt · Sequence · Redirect
DOC.02

The U.I.C. terminal is a sequential command system. You do not type one long command. You type a short keyword, press ENTER, and if it is the first step of a valid access sequence the terminal will respond STEP 1/N ACCEPTED and wait for the next word.

Every access route has a fixed sequence of words. Complete all of them in order and the terminal will grant access and redirect you to the requested page. Enter a wrong word at any point and the sequence RESETS — you start over from step one.

§ 02.1 — Example: Open the Users Directory

> init STEP 1/3 ACCEPTED > access STEP 2/3 ACCEPTED > grant USERS ACCESS GRANTED REDIRECTING...

§ 02.2 — Status Line

Below the prompt is a status line: SEQUENCE, STEP, SECURE. SEQUENCE shows which route you are currently walking; STEP shows how far you are through it. If SEQUENCE reads IDLE, you are at step zero and any first-word will begin a new route.

§ 02.3 — Commands Are Case-Insensitive

init, INIT and Init all match. Spaces at the front and end are trimmed. Only the first word on a line is read — do not chain commands together.

Unknown first words return UNKNOWN COMMAND and do not begin any sequence. Wrong follow-up words trigger INVALID SEQUENCE — RESET.

05

USING THE BULLETIN BOARD

Public Postings · Internal Notices · Updated Periodically
DOC.05

The Bulletin Board is where U.I.C. posts public notices, station memos, staffing updates, project status blurbs, and general information. Think of it as the electronic corkboard for the entire network. It is not a chat, it is not comms — it is one-way from operations to you.

§ 05.1 — Layout

LEFTPosted documents list — scrollable, tap or click a row to open.
RIGHTViewer — the selected document's body, header meta and date.
BOTTOMSearch bar — type keywords to filter the list.

§ 05.2 — Reading a Post

Tap or click any row in the left column to load it into the viewer. The viewer shows the post's meta block (author, station, date, classification) followed by the body text. Posts marked with a 🔒 icon are gated; you will be shown a password prompt instead of the body. If you do not have the code, you do not have the code. Move on.

§ 05.3 — Mobile

On phones the layout stacks — post list on top, viewer below. Tap a row to jump the viewer to it. Search still works and is scoped to whatever is currently loaded. The board is entirely readable on a 320px screen.

§ 05.4 — Posting

Standard operators cannot post to the bulletin. Posts are pushed from station ops; if you have information to submit, route it through your station lead. The bulletin is read-only from any FR-77 node.

Posts are dated in the top-right of each row. Newest is at the top by default. Use the search bar to filter by station name, project code, or author.

TIPS & FAULT CODES

Common Messages · First-Line Recovery
DOC.06
UNKNOWN CMDFirst word was not the start of any valid sequence. Check spelling. Retry.
INVALID SEQYou gave the wrong follow-up word. Sequence is dropped. Restart from step one.
STEP N/M OKProgress. Give the next word in the sequence.
ACCESS GRANTAll steps correct. The page is loading. If the redirect stalls, retype the last word.
NET WARNSelf-test flagged the Systems Network as degraded. Terminal still operates; expect slow page loads.

§ 06.1 — General Advice

• The terminal is stateless between refreshes — reload the page and any half-typed sequence is dropped.
• Do not paste long strings into the prompt. The terminal expects one keyword per ENTER.
• If the CRT tilt makes you seasick on mobile, hold the device still — the tilt tracks touch position.
• Every public page has a link back to the terminal or the home page in the footer.

This manual is public-tier. If you believe you should have restricted access and do not, your clearance has not been provisioned. Contact your station's operations lead. Do not attempt to brute-force unknown sequences — they will simply return UNKNOWN COMMAND and log the attempt to the network.
